- `place_order` returns `True` from the `except` branch, so a failed charge or a failed insert is logged at warning level and then reported to the caller as a successful order; failure must propagate (re-raise or return `False`) so the caller doesn't confirm an order that doesn't exist.
- If `charge()` succeeds but `db.insert` raises, the customer has been charged with no order row recorded, and nothing refunds or voids the charge; the charge and the insert need compensation (refund/void on insert failure) or ordering that makes the failure recoverable, e.g. persisting a pending order before charging and then marking it paid.
- `charge()` is called with no idempotency key, so a retry of `place_order` (made more likely because failures are swallowed and clients may resubmit) can double-charge the customer.
- `except Exception` around both the payment call and the DB write collapses distinct failure modes (card declined, payment gateway timeout with unknown outcome, DB error after a successful charge) into a single warning log; a gateway timeout in particular leaves the charge state unknown and needs reconciliation rather than being treated like an ordinary failure.
